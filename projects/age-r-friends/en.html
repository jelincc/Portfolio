Raise it like a game,
and it becomes a real reward

Users were coming to the app just to use the guide and control features, then leaving. The goal was to hook them with gamification instead, letting them grow a character to full maturity and earn a real reward that would drive up both sticky users and repeat visits. Reward structures were designed differently by region: Korea got the physical product shipped directly, while overseas markets (US, Japan, Vietnam, China) earned points to spend on whatever they wanted in a points shop. I led the "AGE-R Friends" character from 3D modeling through building out the full design system.

Purpose · Hypothesis

Turning device owners into daily app users

Users who came to the app for our device's guide and control features opened it only when they needed to, then left. The starting point was simple: those features alone gave people no reason to visit more often, so we needed something that would turn the app into a daily habit.

If:

Users grow a character by completing missions, and get a free product once it's fully grown

Then:

Visit frequency and time spent will rise, and usage of other in-app features (TV, community) will grow along with them

Context-Specific Character Motion

Feed it a cookie and it bounces up saying "So touched!"; feed it milk and it bounces up saying "I love milk!"; touch it and it tilts its head; go three days without visiting and it squints and retreats into its shell. Designing motion for each of these moments individually is what gave the character its life.

01Designed the reward as an actual product, not just "fun," with different delivery methods by region
RiskGiving away free products or points creates real cost pressure, and some users might churn the moment they've claimed the reward.
ResponseWe offset the cost by requiring repeated missions across many sessions to reach the max level. Reward delivery was also split by region to match local distribution and logistics: direct product shipping in Korea, and points redeemable in a points shop overseas (US, JP, VN, CN).
02Spread missions across multiple in-app features (check-in, community, TV)
RiskMore mission types can wear users out and drag participation down.
ResponseInstead of repetitive clicking, missions were built around real app features like checking in, reading reviews, watching TV, and searching, so that growing a character doubled as learning the app itself.
03Diversified characters across 5 product lines × 7 growth stages
RiskBuilding every character and motion in 3D individually put heavy pressure on resources.
ResponseWe gave each product line its own distinct character so users across different lines each had something different to raise, and designed situational motion on top so the character wouldn't get stale even with repeat visits.
