Objective · Hypothesis

Taking hearing aid adjustments remote, so patients don't have to visit an expert every time

Adjusting a hearing aid's settings meant patients had to visit an expert in person, every single time. For patients living far away or with limited mobility, that visit alone was a major barrier, and for experts, in-person-only consultations capped how many patients they could see in a day. I gathered material on remote device control services, sought advice from experts, mapped out the flow task by task, and built the wireframes.

If:

Video consultation and real-time hearing aid adjustment could happen in a single screen,

Then:

Patients could get the same level of adjustment service as an in-person visit without ever visiting

Research · Interviews

Learning what a real fitting session looks like from a 6-year audiologist

We interviewed an audiologist with 6 years of experience about their real fitting-session experience and what remote adjustment would need to support.

"I explain to patients that hearing aids aren't smart enough to isolate just speech and play back only that."
"We don't adjust by dB. We split sound into loud, medium, and soft, and dial in each one point by point."
"A fitting session usually takes about 10 to 15 minutes, even counting the time to check the history."
1

Audiologists reached for the Hz graph more than the dB graph, and often adjusted in loud/medium/soft groups rather than point by point

2

The history panel (past adjustment records) got used far more often than we expected

3

Most refittings were repeat visits from the same patient, spaced anywhere from a month to a year apart

What we built into the plan and design · We restructured the dB adjustment UI into loud/medium/soft group controls, and split the history panel into its own window so it could be checked mid-consultation. We also added a new note area where audiologists could jot things down during a session.

Decisions & Trade-offs

Hearing graph, real-time adjustment, and video consultation in one screen

Hearing Graph · Fitting

We display left/right ear hearing-test data as a graph, designed so that any value an expert adjusts is reflected instantly.

Video Consultation Integration

We placed the graph/adjustment screen and the video call screen in a single layout, so consultation and adjustment never break stride.

Remote fitting over video call

Shared Design System

We organized recurring components, including Graph, Preset, and Status, by state (active/disabled), turning them into a system that could be reused consistently across screens.

01Placed the graph, adjustment controls, and video call in one layout
RiskCramming all three into one screen raises information density and could make it harder for experts to focus while adjusting.
ResponseWe kept the video call small and made the graph/adjustment UI the main area, so consultation kept flowing while adjustment stayed the focus of the screen.
02Built components as a shared design system from day one
RiskDesigning B2B and B2C screens separately moves faster early on, but consistency tends to fall apart as more screens get added.
ResponseWe organized the Graph, Preset, and Status components by state early, so new screens could scale under the same rules as the product grew.
Round 2 Improvements

Feeding real remote-fitting feedback into a second design pass

After running the service in real remote fittings, we made a second round of UI improvements based on field feedback from audiologists.

1

There was a real risk of misapplying an adjustment by mistake mid-consultation, so we added a flow that clearly shows each step's status: confirm → applying → done.

2

Audiologists frequently referenced a patient's past adjustments during refittings, so we split each patient's fitting history into its own detail panel they could check mid-session.

3

We added a register/edit modal to the customer list so new patients could be registered and existing ones edited without ever leaving the consultation screen.
